Ghostly motion options

Each block shows 2 or 3 options. The recommended option is first and labelled. Everything is built from the project's own textures: mist, rope, slips and card paper. The pale green tint is the Dutchman's lantern light. It shows only while a thing is still forming, so text contrast at rest is unchanged.

Sound pairing: each section has a Sound row. Pick one, then play the motion: the sound plays with it. Every sound is cut from a CC0 Freesound recording and level-matched to about -23 LUFS, so loudness does not decide for you. The frequent sounds (flags, slips, tab) play quieter, as they would in the app. Click anywhere on the page once first; browsers block sound until you do.

1. Flags: the wind along the cord

Hover a flag, then sweep across the line. Option A is a small spring model: the hovered flag catches a gust, the cord carries some of it to its neighbours, and each swing dies down within 3 s after the pointer leaves. A flag that is already moving keeps its motion, so sweeping across looks like one gust travelling along the cord. Keyboard focus gets the same gust.

A. Gust along the cord (recommended)

Spring physics, about 30 lines of JS. Swings 6° at most, with the cloth skewing as it moves. Neighbours swing about 2°. Still again within 3 s.

B. Fading sway (CSS)

One keyframe sway that dies out, about 1.2 s. A restart only happens after it ends, so there is no jump. Neighbours do not move. CSS plus 3 lines of JS.

C. Breeze while hovered

A steady sway while the pointer is over the flag, which stops when it leaves (ends its current swing). Plainest option. Nothing lingers.

2. About and Passwords slips: a draft through the door

The same wind on the nailed slips, which swing around their nail. Paper is lighter than cloth, so the swing is bigger: 5° at most. The slip next to it flutters a little too.

A. Spring flutter (recommended)

Same script as flags A. Each slip has its own feel: About swings slow and heavy; Passwords swings quicker with a paper flutter. Still within 4 s.

B. Fading sway (CSS)

Same as flags B.

C. Today: straighten and drop 4 px

No wind. Shown for comparison.

3. Drink cards, first load: ghosting in

The drinks come from local data and are ready at once, so a skeleton has nothing real to wait for. Its "loading" is staging: the fog wisps show for 300 ms, then the cards condense out of them. The whole sequence ends in under 1 s. It runs once per page load, not on a tab switch or language change.

A. Smoke skull, wisps, then spectral materialise (your pick)

An aged, pitted skull condenses out of drifting fog over 2 s, holds, then the fog takes it back as it drifts up (1.4 s). Card wisps appear as it goes, and the cards condense out of them. About 4 s in all, once per page load.

B. Fog rolls off the table

One mist bank covers the grid and drifts off to the right while every card sharpens from a 4 px blur. 0.9 s, no stagger.

C. Plain fade

Opacity only, 250 ms. Shown for comparison.

4. Tab switch: ghost glide with many cards

24 cards with the beers first, as in the real All Drinks list. So going from All to Beer moves no card: the beers keep their seats and only the rest go. To make every switch visible, every card that stays does a ghost pass in a wave from the top left (capped at 600 ms across the grid). Cards that move also glide. With reduced motion, both options are a plain fade.

A. Lingering afterimage (recommended)

Each kept card dims to a pale ghost and back (900 ms), and a pale afterimage rises 12 px off it and fades over 1.4 s. Leaving cards fade as ghosts; new ones condense. About 2 s in all with the wave.

B. Spectral trail

Each kept card goes pale and throws three echoes upward that fade one after another; a card that moves leaves them along its path. About 2.2 s in all with the wave.

5. Page arrival: through the fog

From the landing page to the tavern, and back on Log Out. It uses cross-document View Transitions: one CSS rule plus the keyframes. Firefox loads the page plainly.

A. Fog passes over (recommended)

The old page sinks into the fog (700 ms), a slow bank of mist passes, and the new page condenses pale, then solid. 1.4 s in all.
